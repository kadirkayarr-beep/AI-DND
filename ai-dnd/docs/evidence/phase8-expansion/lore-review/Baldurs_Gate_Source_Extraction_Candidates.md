# Baldur's Gate source extraction candidates

Status of every record: **UNREVIEWED**. Nothing here is approved canon, a production pack, or an activation.

Adventures in Faerûn chapter 6 is the current-authority text used here. Descent into Avernus is supplemental, and it dates its own city portrait to 1492 DR. Where the two disagree, both claims are kept. The adventure's disappearances, cult war, and possible endings are not campaign history.

Inside a future reviewed profile, the model may propose minor locals, crews, arguments, and small incidents. It may not rewrite the Council, the Watch, the Flaming Fist, or the city's history. Python keeps validation, mechanics, and persistence.

Printed folios for Adventures in Faerûn were not visible. Those printed pages are SOURCE NOT VERIFIED. Descent into Avernus PDF 161 begins with the visible folio 160.

## Essential current-candidate facts

### BG-F-001

- Normalized fact: Baldur's Gate stands where major Sword Coast roads meet the wide, deep Chionthar River, so seagoing ships can reach Gray Harbor. The city grew from the hero Balduran's foundation into a merchant city.
- Source: Forgotten Realms: Adventures in Faerûn, chapter 6. PDF page: 270.
- Printed page: SOURCE NOT VERIFIED. Section: Baldur's Gate opening.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: a river port and road hub.
- Prohibited: moving the harbor or renaming the river.
- Evidence: the chapter opening. Descent into Avernus tells a longer founding story, kept as BG-H-001.
- Conflict: none on the geography. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-002

- Normalized fact: The city is divided into the Upper City of wealthy patriars, the Lower City of working people and trade, and the Outer City of refugees and poor residents. Walls and gates separate them.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 270 and PDF 281.
- Section: opening; Baldur's Gate Gazetteer.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: class geography and gate checks.
- Prohibited: merging the three districts or removing the walls.
- Evidence: both pages. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-003

- Normalized fact: The Council of Four governs trade law. It has three dukes and one grand duke. It enacts laws, directs the Flaming Fist and the navy, and collects taxes.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 273.
- Section: Power in Baldur's Gate, Council of Four.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: ducal government and tax collectors.
- Prohibited: a king, a democracy, or a fifth voting duke as established government.
- Evidence: the section. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-004

- Normalized fact: The current grand duke is Ulder Ravengard. He rose as supreme marshal of the Flaming Fist, won election as grand duke, and kept command of the Fist. His attention to government has left the company harsher and more corrupt. An art caption shows him addressing the people.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 273 and 274.
- Section: Council of Four; The Flaming Fist.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current in this book. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: Ravengard as the living grand duke.
- Prohibited: reporting him missing, dead, or replaced.
- Evidence: the office paragraph and the caption "Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard addresses the people of Baldur's Gate."
- Conflict: C-001. Descent into Avernus chapter 1 says he is missing in Elturel. That claim is BG-A-001, not this fact. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-005

- Normalized fact: Adventures in Faerûn names the other council members as Duke Dillar Portyr, described as distant and ineffective; Duke Brevek Faenor, a fiery priest; and Duke Belynne Stelmane.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 273.
- Section: Council of Four.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current in this book. Knowledge: public that they are dukes. Faenor's stat block is mechanics, not lore to narrate as numbers.
- Allowed use: these three as the sitting dukes beside Ravengard.
- Prohibited: swapping in a different duke as established government.
- Evidence: one sentence listing the three.
- Conflict: C-002. Descent into Avernus spells the name Dillard Portyr and seats Duke Thalamra Vanthampur instead of Faenor. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-006

- Normalized fact: Duke Belynne Stelmane is possessed by a mind flayer and struggles for control of her body and actions.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 273. Descent into Avernus, PDF 163, says a mind flayer caused her apparent seizure and that her aides do not know.
- Section: Council of Four; Duke Belynne Stelmane.
- Authority: current setting, confirmed in both books. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: **restricted**.
- Allowed use: she can appear as a duke with slowed speech. The cause stays hidden.
- Prohibited: telling players she is possessed, curing her by narration, or killing the mind flayer as a past event.
- Evidence: both passages. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-007

- Normalized fact: The Parliament of Peers is an advisory body of fifty politicians, mostly patriars. It meets in the High Hall in the Upper City. It recommends policy and does not enact laws. The council often follows those recommendations and uses members to carry edicts out.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 273.
- Section: Parliament of Peers.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: political errands and patronage.
- Prohibited: letting the parliament pass law on its own, or changing its size.
- Evidence: the section. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-008

- Normalized fact: The Watch is paid by the patriars, based in the Watch Citadel in the Upper City, and tasked with that district. At dusk it expels everyone except residents, designated guests, and authorized servants, who carry a Watch token. It staffs the Old Wall.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 273.
- Section: The Watch.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: a gate closing at dusk and a token check.
- Prohibited: sending the Watch to police the whole city as its job.
- Evidence: the section. The sentence was cut at the end of the extract after "authorizing the"; the token rule itself was readable. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-009

- Normalized fact: The Flaming Fist is a mercenary company paid by the Council of Four to defend the city, patrol, and punish lawbreakers. Its headquarters is the Seatower of Balduran in Gray Harbor. It defers to the Watch in the Upper City, is most active in the Lower City, rarely enters the Outer City except in force, and staffs Wyrm's Rock to collect taxes and raise the bridges of Wyrm's Crossing at night.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 274.
- Section: The Flaming Fist.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: a rough, uneven mercenary patrol.
- Prohibited: making the Fist a holy order, or giving it routine authority over the Upper City.
- Evidence: the section. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-010

- Normalized fact: A thieves' guild so widespread that it is known only as the Guild profits from citizens and visitors. Descent into Avernus adds that the Guild oversees almost every organized criminal act, from dockside gambling to blackmail at patriar parties.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 270; Descent into Avernus, PDF 159.
- Section: chapter opening; gazetteer opening.
- Authority: current setting for the Guild's existence; the Avernus scope sentence is supplemental and dated with the 1492 portrait.
- Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public that the Guild exists; its membership is restricted.
- Allowed use: organized street crime as a pressure, with unnamed minor crooks.
- Prohibited: naming a new Guild king as official lore, or wiping the Guild out.
- Evidence: both sentences. No leader was extracted. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-011

- Normalized fact: Inner gates between Upper and Lower City are Baldur's Gate itself and the Patriar Gates: Gond Gate, Heap Gate, Manor Gate, and Sea Gate. Outer wall gates include Basilisk Gate toward the Coast Way, Black Dragon Gate on the road north to Waterdeep, Citadel Gate into the Watch's Citadel Streets, and Cliffgate toward Tumbledown and the cemeteries of Dusthawk Hill.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 281.
- Section: Baldur's Gate Gazetteer, gates.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: travel through a named gate.
- Prohibited: inventing a new principal gate as official geography.
- Evidence: the gazetteer lists. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-012

- Normalized fact: Patriars are the elite families of the Upper City. Their manors are opulent town houses. Pedigree is claimed and often not proved. Some families patronize public works; some rumors say some houses hide devil worship or Dead Three contact. The worst cases are hidden, not a public fact about every family.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 275.
- Section: People of Baldur's Gate, Patriars.
- Authority: current setting. Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate. Knowledge: public for the class; a specific house's secret is restricted.
- Allowed use: a proud, secretive noble household.
- Prohibited: declaring every patriar a cultist, or creating a new patriar house as official nobility.
- Evidence: the section. The Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide adds that the Shattershields are a dwarven patriar family. That detail is supplemental, guide PDF 47, and was not repeated in the Adventures pages read.
- Conflict: none required. The Shattershield line stays optional until a reviewer accepts the 2015 guide beside the current chapter. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-013

- Normalized fact: Adventures in Faerûn says cultists of sinister gods promote murder, tyranny, and necromancy in secret, and that some citizens secretly worship fiends or are fiends in disguise. Descent into Avernus says people may worship any deity if they avoid violence and disruption of trade, and that open worship of the Dead Three is frowned on.
- Source: Adventures in Faerûn, PDF 270; Descent into Avernus, PDF 167.
- Section: chapter opening; Religion.
- Authority: current danger from Adventures; the tolerance rule is supplemental, 1492 gazetteer.
- Class: setting fact. Time: current candidate for hidden cult danger. Knowledge: the tolerance is public; a hidden cult cell is restricted.
- Allowed use: unease, graffiti, and a small secret meeting.
- Prohibited: making Bane, Bhaal, or Myrkul the city religion, or staging the adventure's Dead Three dungeon as history.
- Evidence: both passages. The specific financed murder plot is BG-A-003. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-014

- Normalized fact: The 1492 gazetteer names the Unrolling Scroll as Oghma's shrine in the Upper City, a Tymora temple in the Upper City used informally by adventurers, and the Water Queen's House as Umberlee's temple on the docks. It says Siamorphe's old temple was burned in an early uprising and its site is forgotten.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, 2019, PDF 168.
- Printed page: folio alignment suggests printed 167; the visible method is weaker here, so printed page is SOURCE NOT VERIFIED pending a folio check. PDF page: 168.
- Section: Baldur's Gate Gazetteer, religion.
- Authority: supplemental. Class: setting fact inside the 1492 portrait. Time: **unknown** for 1501, because Adventures chapter 6 did not restate these houses.
- Knowledge: public for the standing shrines; the lost Siamorphe vault is rumor.
- Allowed use: none as a 1501 certainty until review. A reviewer may accept them as supplemental.
- Prohibited: inventing replacements, or declaring the lost temple found.
- Evidence: the religion pages. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-015

- Normalized fact: The 1492 gazetteer gives the population as 125,000, predominantly humans.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, PDF 160, coat-of-arms block.
- Authority: supplemental. Time: 1492 figure. The 1501 population was not in the Adventures pages read, so it is **unknown**.
- Knowledge: public for that book.
- Allowed use: a large human-majority city, without quoting 125,000 as the 1501 census.
- Prohibited: updating the number by invention.
- Evidence: the gazetteer statistic. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-016

- Normalized fact: In the 1492 gazetteer, Baldur's Gate trades with Waterdeep and Neverwinter, contends with Amn, and belongs to the Lords' Alliance largely because of that contention. Elturgard and Cormyr are described as disdainful neighbors. The Elturel paragraph is explicitly "prior to the events described in this adventure."
- Source: Descent into Avernus, PDF 161 and 162.
- Authority: supplemental. Class: setting description of neighbors, with the Elturel sentence already marked as the pre-adventure condition.
- Time: 1492 portrait. Whether every alliance detail is unchanged in 1501 is unknown.
- Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: trade north along the coast, and tension with Amn, as supplemental color.
- Prohibited: starting a war with Amn or Elturgard as established history.
- Evidence: "Neighbors, Rivals, and Allies." Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-F-017

- Normalized fact: The Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide says trade between the Upper and Lower Cities is taxed at the gate called Baldur's Gate, and that other gates are for patriars and their retinues. People without a patriar, livery, or letter of employment must use that gate.
- Source: Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide, 2015, PDF 47.
- Printed page: SOURCE NOT VERIFIED.
- Section: Baldur's Gate, Lower City passage.
- Authority: supplemental. Class: setting fact. Time: 2015 description, compatible with the later gate list but not restated in the same words.
- Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: a tax queue at the named gate.
- Prohibited: opening every inner gate to untaxed trade as established law.
- Evidence: the guide. Review: UNREVIEWED.

## Optional historical detail

### BG-H-001

- Normalized fact: Centuries ago Balduran returned to the village of Gray Harbor with wealth. Friends and family built businesses, docks, and a wall. One passage was called Baldur's Gate, and within a generation that name became the town's name.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, PDF 159, History of Baldur's Gate. Adventures in Faerûn PDF 270 agrees that Balduran founded the city and that it outgrew his vision.
- Authority: historical. Time: founding era. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: the gate's name and civic pride.
- Prohibited: returning the city to a village, or bringing Balduran back as a present ruler.
- Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-H-002

- Normalized fact: After violence between the Upper and Lower Cities, elected rulers called dukes, collectively the Council of Four, were created. Later the warrior Eltan united mercenary companies under the Flaming Fist.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, PDF 160.
- Authority: historical origin of institutions that still exist. Time: historical. Knowledge: public.
- Allowed use: the names of the institutions.
- Prohibited: restoring Eltan as the present commander. The present marshal in the current book is Ravengard.
- Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-H-003

- Normalized fact: During and after the Time of Troubles, Bhaal fathered the Bhaalspawn. Sarevok, of the Iron Throne, tried to start a war with Amn and become the new Lord of Murder. Another Bhaalspawn and allies stopped him. The gazetteer says Bhaal's faithful have menaced the city since.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, PDF 160.
- Authority: historical. Time: past crisis. Knowledge: public as history; living cult cells are restricted.
- Allowed use: old fear of the Lord of Murder.
- Prohibited: replaying Sarevok's plot as a current, already-resolved campaign event, or deciding who the surviving Bhaalspawn is.
- Review: UNREVIEWED.

## Adventure possibilities (not history)

### BG-A-001

- Normalized constraint: Descent into Avernus begins after Elturel has vanished into Avernus. Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard is missing, the gates are sealed against Elturgard refugees, and the Flaming Fist drafts adventurers because it lacks his leadership.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, 2019, PDF 6. The gazetteer on PDF 160 says the city is described as of 1492 DR.
- Class: adventure starting condition. Knowledge: DM plot.
- Prohibited: treating Elturel as already destroyed, or Ravengard as already lost, in the 1501 campaign.
- Conflict: C-001. Adventures in Faerûn shows him in office. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-A-002

- Normalized constraint: In the adventure, Duke Thalamra Vanthampur, a disciple of Zariel, finances Dead Three cultists with stolen treasure so the Flaming Fist can be discredited and she can claim the grand dukedom.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, PDF 6 and 163.
- Class: adventure possibility. Time: module plot. Her seat is not in the Adventures council list.
- Prohibited: making her grand duke, dead, or exposed as a completed campaign event. Her 1501 whereabouts are unknown.
- Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-A-003

- Normalized constraint: Chapter 1's murders, the Dungeon of the Dead Three, Vanthampur Villa, and the later hell war are scenario locations and outcomes.
- Source: Descent into Avernus contents, PDF 4, and the chapter 1 overview.
- Class: adventure possibility.
- Prohibited: writing those battles, deaths, or rescues into the city's past.
- The separate setting fact that secret cults exist is BG-F-013. Review: UNREVIEWED.

### BG-A-004

- Normalized constraint: Chapter 5 offers endings in which heroes may return from Avernus and deal with leftover villains in Baldur's Gate.
- Source: Descent into Avernus, PDF 147–150, from the contents scan and the escape-chapter headings.
- Class: possible outcomes.
- Prohibited: choosing an ending as history.
- Review: UNREVIEWED.

## Safe emergent categories, after a reviewed profile exists

Ordinary categories that fit the boundaries: a Fist shakedown in the Lower City, a Watch token argument at dusk, a Guild pickpocket, a patriar servant's private grudge, a gate-tax delay, a dock rumor, a crew protecting one block. Python still accepts or rejects each proposal.

Unsafe categories: a new duke, Ravengard's death or abduction, Elturel's fall, a coup by Vanthampur, a new state religion, a published Bhaalspawn identity, or a mind-flayer revelation about Stelmane.

## Forbidden political, religious, and historical mutations

Do not replace the Council of Four, unseat Ravengard, merge the Watch with the Flaming Fist, legalize the Dead Three, erase the Guild, open the Upper City at night, or adopt any Descent into Avernus victory or disaster as something that has already happened.
